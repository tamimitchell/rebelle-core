# Rebelle Rally — imagery

What the rally shows, distilled from the five **14 · Imagery** cards of the
Field Glass design system (Claude Design project `b992548a…`, snapshot in
[`design-sync/14-imagery/`](design-sync/14-imagery/)), with detail from the
project's longer draft (`uploads/imagery.md`). [`voice.md`](voice.md) is what
the rally says; this is what it shows, for everyone downstream of the camera —
choosing, cropping, placing, captioning. Themes, not rules: the field
photographers' taste leads, and where their eye and this page disagree, their
eye is probably right.

**Real beats overly curated.** A course scout's shaky phone video, dusty and
true, can outrank the most polished frame. When two images argue, the honest
one usually wins.

## The eight frames

What the camera keeps coming back to. Not a shot list, not a quota.

| # | Frame | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Terrain is the protagonist** | The landscape dwarfs the vehicle: an enormous route, a small capable you. The desert is the hero case, not the only one. |
| 02 | **Vehicle against vastness** | The signature frame: one small truck, an immense land. The scale shot usually tells more than the tight beauty shot. |
| 03 | **Tools on the tailgate** | Maps, compass, roadbook, plotter in use: the analog still life the sport owns. |
| 04 | **Women, working** | Capability over glamour: a tire coming off, a heading plotted, papers under a dome light. The work is the beauty. |
| 05 | **Landmark as waypoint** | Place is not scenery; it is how you find the checkpoint. |
| 06 | **Camp at night** | Star fields, lantern pools, the long table: the warm counterweight. |
| 07 | **The aerial read** | A vehicle tracing its line through terrain: the navigator's view made literal. |
| 08 | **The crew and the craft** | The rally documents its own makers the way it documents competitors. |

## The light

Warm western light leads, and the day has an arc the imagery keeps:
**cold dawn starts → hard honest noon → golden hour, the brand's hour →
lantern and headlamp night.** Hard light is honest: glare, dust on the lens and
weather rolling in are documented, not corrected away. Working light — the
dome-lit navigator before dawn, the headlamp wrench session — is a beloved
register.

## Treatment

- **True colour leads.** The default is faithful: warm light, real dust,
  honest colour.
- **The warm duotone ✅** — a sepia, warm-mono treatment — is a named register
  for series covers and title cards (*"Everyone starts somewhere."*). Openers
  only, with a light hand.
- **Motion reads as travel:** pans, dust plumes, distance being covered. Never
  speed as the subject.
- **Scrims are instruments.** Type sits on a photograph only over a directional
  scrim, and the photograph keeps at least 60% of itself clear.
- **Crops keep the point.** Keep the vastness in vehicle-against-vastness, keep
  the tools legible, keep her eyes.
- **Sponsor brights stay inside the photograph** — a liveried truck, a flag on
  a dune — never in the chrome or the caption around it.

## What doesn't belong

- **Nothing staged, stocked or performed.** The dust stays in frame. Fake is
  the only disqualifier, not fidelity: shaky phone video from the course
  belongs. Real triumph — arms raised at the finish, tears at Rebellation — is
  celebrated big, because it happened.
- **No adrenaline framing.** Endurance and precision, never a pretend sprint.
- **No gadget hero shots.** The analog tools are the pride; the technology
  stays behind the tape.

## Placing and credit

- **Credit, by name, is the one hard rule ◆.** Every post, surface and
  deliverable that carries a photograph: `Photo: Regine Trias · 2025`.
- **Day and place are worth keeping when you have them.** Galleries and recaps
  run by day (Tech → Prologue → Days 1–8 → Rebellation). Never guess one; an
  unplaced photograph still earns its slot.
- **Partner images ship finished ◆:** edited, no raw files; galleries land by
  day during the rally.
- **Choose like a photo editor:** which frame does this surface need, at which
  hour of the day's arc?

## When you're unsure

In order: **Is it real? Would the rally recognize itself? Is the photographer
credited?** Yes, yes and yes — run it.
