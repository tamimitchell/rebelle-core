# The map, and why it looks this way

Rebelle is run on paper maps and a compass. So the live map is paper too. Not a satellite photo, not a dark screen with glowing lines: a printed sheet, the kind the competitors unfold on the hood at dawn.

**Two sheets, one language.** Atlas is the full sheet, for the website. It carries a ghost of the satellite imagery for washes and rock grain, the small contour lines, the tracks, and the names of peaks and lakes. Terrain Paper is the quiet sheet, for video. Same colours, no imagery, fewer lines, no labels, so titles and panels can sit on it without fighting for attention. Everything else about them is identical, and a viewer can switch between them without the map moving.

**Colour means height.** The sheet goes from playa bone on the valley floor up to rust on the summits. The range fits itself to whatever ground is in view, so the Glamis dunes and the Nevada high country each get the full sweep instead of one flat brown. Pine green appears only where there are real trees. The land-cover data calls the whole Mojave "scrub", so we do not paint scrub.

**Relief is pressed in, not drawn on.** The hills are lit from one direction and shaded in copper where the light falls, dark slate where the rock is rugged. Six times of day move that light. Right now that is a control on the demo; later it could follow the real clock.

**Roads are pale.** From the air, a worn track reads as a lighter line on the desert, and that is how it reads here. Water is cyan, the one annotation colour on the sheet, and it stays quiet.

**The paper is what you look at. The glass is what you look through.** Everything on top of the sheet speaks in the tracker's voice, navy and cyan: checkpoints as the rally's own marks (green flag, blue square, black diamond), each in full colour whether or not it has been reached; the followed team as a door-plate number; its trail in cyan, fading behind it like dust. Start, finish and base camp are small navy glyphs. The panels around the edge are Field Glass, the instrument look from the broadcast. The map itself is never glass.

**What is invented, for now.** The demo day, the route, and every team on it are made up. The real tracker will draw a team's own breadcrumb rather than a line through the checkpoints, and real checkpoints are scattered across the day, with names like 3A or 13B depending on the track. Whole-field views exist but are switched off until there is a live feed to drive them.

**Questions for you.** Does this read as the rally's map, or as somebody's map of the rally? Do the checkpoint marks match what a team sees on the ground? Is there anything on the sheet you would take away?
