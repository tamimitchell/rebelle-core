🟡 Sizes — deliberately off the ramp: each is a measured optical or ergonomic value, not a multiple
--hit-min · 44pxThe mobile hit-target floor. A floor, not a size — never ship below it.
--icon-inline · 17pxTabler beside a label, optically matched to 11.5px body.
--icon-mark · 20pxA card's own mark, standing alone with no ring.
--icon-chip · 12pxInside a chip, against 9px mono.
Derived, not invented. An audit of 77 files found 30 distinct padding values and 26 distinct gaps — every integer from 1 to 46, and no scale at all. These eight steps are that audit's frequency peaks rounded onto one ramp, so adopting them moves most existing values by 0–2px instead of redrawing the system. Two rhythms, one scale. 4–12 is micro: distances inside a single row, where the eye reads one object. 16–48 is macro: distances between blocks, where the eye moves. Borrowing across the two — a 24px gap inside a row, a 6px gap between blocks — is what makes a layout feel arbitrary even when every value is on-scale. The register is the source of truth. Button, Chip and the list marks are on the scale; --sp-16 at lg was not cosmetic, since 14px measured 42px tall and missed the hit floor. Specimen cards in this system are measured frames at fixed viewports, so their own padding fits the card — read the register, not the card.
